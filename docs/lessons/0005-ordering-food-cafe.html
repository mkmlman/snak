Lesson 0005 Spoken Danish ~12 min

Ordering food at a cafe

Order coffee, pastry, or a smørrebrød — and handle the checkout conversation.

Today's win

Jeg vil gerne have en kaffe, tak. → Værsgo. → Mange tak!

01 Why this matters

Cafes are everywhere in Copenhagen. Even a simple order in Danish changes the interaction — Danes genuinely appreciate the effort.

02 Key pattern: Jeg vil gerne have

Pattern

Jeg vil gerne have + [item] = "I would like to have" — the standard polite ordering phrase. Vil gerne softens the request (≈ "would like to").

03 Phrase cards

Jeg vil gerne have en kaffe

I'd like a coffee

Black coffee default. Specify: en kaffe med mælk (with milk), en cortado, en latte.

En croissant, tak

A croissant, please

For food items, you can skip Jeg vil gerne have and just say the item + tak.

Værsgo

Here you go / please (handing something)

You'll hear this constantly. Staff say it when handing your order. Also used when offering something: Værsgo.

Skal det være noget andet?

Would you like anything else?

Standard follow-up question from staff. Answer: Nej tak, det var det hele (No thanks, that's all).

Det var det hele

That's all / that was it

Use this to signal you're done ordering.

God dag!

Have a good day!

Standard friendly send-off. Reply: I lige måde! (likewise!).

05 Model dialogue

You
Hej! Jeg vil gerne have en kaffe, tak.
Hi! I'd like a coffee, please.

Barista
Værsgo. Skal det være noget andet?
Here you go. Would you like anything else?

You
Nej tak, det var det hele.
No thanks, that's all.

Barista
Det bliver 35 kroner.
That'll be 35 kroner.

You
Tak! God dag!
Thanks! Have a good day!

06 Retrieval practice

1. "I'd like a coffee"
2. "Here you go" (handing something)
3. "That's all" (done ordering)

07 Real-world task

Before next lesson

  2. When they hand it to you, listen for Værsgo.
  3. Say Mange tak! God dag! when leaving.
