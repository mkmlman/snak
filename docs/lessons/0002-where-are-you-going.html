Lesson 0002 Spoken Danish ~10 min

Where are you going?

Ask and answer about where someone is headed — a core small-talk move in the building, on the street, or at work.

Today's win

Hvor skal du hen? → Jeg skal på arbejde / i Netto / hjem.

01 Why this matters

Danes ask Hvor skal du hen? the way English speakers ask "How's it going?" — it's a greeting ritual, not a serious interrogation. Having a reply ready makes you sound natural.

02 Key pattern: at skulle + direction

Pattern

Jeg skal + til / på / i + [place]
Jeg skal på arbejde (I'm going to work) · Jeg skal i Netto (I'm going to Netto) · Jeg skal til lægen (I'm going to the doctor)

03 Phrase cards

Hvor skal du hen?

Where are you going?

Word-by-word: hvor (where) + skal (shall/must) + du (you) + hen (towards) · Note: hen adds directionality. Very common in spoken Danish.

Jeg skal på arbejde

I'm going to work

Pattern: på + arbejde (work). Use på with workplaces and institutions.

Jeg skal i Netto

I'm going to Netto (the supermarket)

Pattern: i + shop/store name. Also: i skole (to school), i byen (to town/the city).

Jeg skal hjem

I'm going home

Note: hjem (homewards) is an adverb, no preposition needed. Jeg er hjemme = I am (at) home.

Jeg skal til træning

I'm going to training / the gym

Pattern: til + activities, events, people. Jeg skal til lægen (to the doctor), til fest (to a party).

Hvad med dig?

What about you?

Bounce-back phrase — use this instead of og dig? when talking about plans.

05 Model dialogue

Neighbour
Hej! Hvor skal du hen?
Hi! Where are you going?

You
Hej! Jeg skal på arbejde. Hvad med dig?
Hi! I'm going to work. What about you?

Neighbour
Jeg skal i Netto. Hej hej!
I'm going to Netto. Bye!

You
Hej hej!
Bye!

06 Retrieval practice

1. "Where are you going?"
2. "I'm going to work"
3. "I'm going home"
