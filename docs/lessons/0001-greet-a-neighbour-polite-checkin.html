Lesson 0001 Spoken Danish ~10 min

Greet a neighbour (polite check-in)

One skill: open a polite hello with someone in your building, ask how they are, answer about yourself, and close cleanly — in spoken Danish.

Audio: Your browser speaks the Danish for you. Quality depends on the Danish voice on your device, so it might not match how Copenhageners actually sound. Listen to neighbours, not the robot.
Today’s win

By the end you can run this loop out loud without looking: Hej → Hvordan går det? → Det går godt → Og dig? → Hvad hedder du? → Jeg hedder … → Tak.

01 Why this matters

Your mission is everyday Danish with neighbours — not exams, not formal writing. The first real social move is a polite check-in. Get this smooth and every later lesson (prices, directions, small talk) attaches to a face you've already greeted.

02 One grammar lever: du vs De

Danish used to have a formal De (like German Sie or French vous). In modern Denmark, you almost never need it.

Register

Du / dig / din = standard "you / your" for everyone — neighbours, staff, strangers, even the queen.
De / Dem / Deres = very formal, essentially retired in daily life. Skip it.

Word order is V2 (verb second), like English: Jeg hedder Flint ≈ "I am called Flint."

03 Phrase cards (≤10 new items)

Hej

Hello / hi

Register: safe everywhere · The universal Danish opener. Hej hej is also common as a casual goodbye.

Hvordan går det?

How are you? / How's it going?

Word-by-word: hvordan (how) + går (goes) + det (it) · Also hear: Hvordan har du det? (how are you feeling?) — same idea.

Det går godt

I'm doing well / It's going well

Word-by-word: det (it) + går (goes) + godt (well) · Also hear: Fint (fine), Super (great). Stick with Det går godt as your default.

Og dig?

And you?

Use after answering about yourself — bounce the check-in back.

Hvad hedder du?

What is your name?

Word-by-word: hvad (what) + hedder (are-called) + du (you) · Lit. "What are you called?"

Jeg hedder Flint

My name is Flint

Word-by-word: jeg (I) + hedder (am-called) + Flint · Swap in any name after hedder.

Tak

Thank you

Register: everyday polite — use it constantly. Mange tak (many thanks) for extra warmth. Tak skal du have (thanks to you) for emphasis.

Selv tak

You're welcome (lit. "thanks yourself")

Standard reply when someone thanks you. Det var så lidt (it was so little) is also common.

05 Model dialogue

You
Hej!
Hello.

Neighbour
Hej! Hvordan går det?
Hello. How are you?

You
Det går godt, tak! Og dig?
I'm fine, thanks! And you?

Neighbour
Også godt, tak.
Also good, thanks.

You
Hvad hedder du?
What is your name?

Neighbour
Jeg hedder Mette. Og dig?
My name is Mette. And you?

You
Jeg hedder Flint. Mange tak!
My name is Flint. Thanks very much!

Say it out loud three times — cover the glosses on the second pass.

06 Retrieval practice

Equal-length options so wording doesn't leak the answer.

1. “How are you?” in Danish
2. “I'm doing well” (your reply)
3. “What is your name?”
4. “My name is Flint”
5. “Thank you” in Danish

07 Production (cover + speak)

Without looking up, say in Danish:

  1. Hello.
  2. How are you?
  3. I'm fine, thanks. And you?
  4. What is your name?
  5. My name is Flint. Thanks very much!

Then flip back and score yourself honestly. Redo any miss out loud twice.
